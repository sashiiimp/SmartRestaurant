# SmartRestaurant: ML evaluation (actual results)

These numbers were produced on 4 Oct 2026 by `flask smartrest train` (sections 2 and 3, copied from the stored
`model_versions.metrics` of model `v20261004-1`) and `flask smartrest evaluate` (sections 4 to 6) on the synthetic
dataset (763 days x 10 items, 1 Sep 2024 to 3 Oct 2026, seed 42, generated by `app/seed/generate.py` with the
corrected holiday calendar of section 7). Nothing here is planned or estimated. Re-running the same commands on the
same data reproduces them (fixed seeds, pinned library versions).

**Read this first.** The data is synthetic. Its effects (weekday pattern, Poya days and other holidays, promotions,
rain, trend) were written by us, so the model is being asked to recover structure we put there. The results show
that the pipeline works, that a model with contextual features beats a same-weekday-last-week rule *when such
structure exists*, and which kinds of context it can use. They do not show how accurate the system would be in a
real restaurant, or how large these effects are in reality.

## 1. Set-up

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Target | quantity sold per menu item per day |
| Hold-out | last 56 days (2026-08-09 to 2026-10-03), chronological, never used for fitting; 560 item-days |
| Training rows | 6,790 (2024-09-01 to 2026-08-08; each item's first 28 days are dropped for lag features) |
| Features | weekday, weekend, month, public holiday, Poya day, school holiday, promotion discount, rain, temperature, lags 7/14/21/28, rolling means, item |
| Weather in every test period | seasonal estimate computed from the data before that period (what the model sees when forecasting tomorrow) |
| Models | naive (same weekday last week), seasonal mean of the last 4 same weekdays, Random Forest (300 trees), XGBoost (300 trees, depth 5) |
| Selection | lowest hold-out MAE; the shipped model is then refit on all history |

## 2. Results on the hold-out

| Model | MAE | RMSE | MAPE % | WAPE % | R2 | Bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive_lag7 (baseline) | 13.88 | 19.11 | 24.8 | 21.9 | 0.782 | 0.65 |
| seasonal_mean4 | 10.85 | 14.86 | 20.0 | 17.1 | 0.868 | 0.95 |
| random_forest | 9.66 | 13.86 | 17.4 | 15.2 | 0.885 | 0.54 |
| **xgboost (selected)** | **9.20** | **13.06** | **16.6** | **14.5** | **0.898** | -0.38 |

- XGBoost beat the naive baseline on MAE by 33.7% (9.20 vs 13.88), so the acceptance criterion S1 is met.
- **Noise floor.** The generator's noise-free expected demand has an MAE of 8.26 against the sampled sales over the
  hold-out. No model can do better than that on this data. XGBoost is within 0.93 portions of it.
- Overall WAPE 14.5% meets the planned target of about 15% (a target, not a promise; it depends on the generator's
  noise level). Bias is close to zero for the learned models. MAPE excludes no rows (no actual of zero).

## 3. Live monitoring check

Admin > Monitoring > Forecast accuracy compares stored forecasts with actual sales. The 56 backfilled days use the
copy of the model fitted before those days (`eval_model` in the artifact), so they are out-of-sample and match the
hold-out. (An earlier version used the refit model and showed a flattering MAE; that was in-sample and was corrected.)

## 4. Rolling-origin cross-validation (`flask smartrest evaluate`)

One hold-out window can be lucky or unlucky. Six consecutive 28-day test windows (19 Apr to 3 Oct 2026) were
therefore evaluated, each with every model trained only on the days before its window (expanding window) and with the
estimated weather. Each line is one window; the floor is the noise-free MAE of that window.

| Test window | naive | seasonal mean | Random Forest | XGBoost | winner | noise floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 19 Apr - 16 May | 16.59 | 13.75 | **10.86** | 11.10 | RF | 8.91 |
| 17 May - 13 Jun | 13.57 | 10.56 | **8.83** | 8.84 | RF | 7.85 |
| 14 Jun - 11 Jul | 12.19 | 9.93 | **9.00** | 9.04 | RF | 8.06 |
| 12 Jul - 8 Aug | 13.05 | 10.28 | 9.59 | **9.27** | XGB | 8.56 |
| 9 Aug - 5 Sep | 13.32 | 10.73 | 10.17 | **9.33** | XGB | 8.25 |
| 6 Sep - 3 Oct | 14.45 | 10.96 | 9.31 | **9.11** | XGB | 8.28 |

| Model | MAE mean | MAE std | WAPE % mean | WAPE std | windows beating naive | windows won |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive_lag7 | 13.86 | 1.53 | 22.0 | 2.3 | - | 0 |
| seasonal_mean4 | 11.04 | 1.38 | 17.5 | 2.0 | 6 of 6 | 0 |
| random_forest | 9.63 | 0.77 | 15.3 | 0.8 | 6 of 6 | 3 |
| xgboost | 9.45 | 0.83 | 15.0 | 1.1 | 6 of 6 | 3 |

- **Both learned models beat both baselines in every window**, by 3.2 to 5.7 portions of MAE against naive. This result does
  not depend on the choice of hold-out.
- **Random Forest and XGBoost are not meaningfully different.** Each wins three windows, and the gap in mean MAE (0.18)
  is far smaller than the spread between windows (0.8). Selecting XGBoost on the single hold-out is reasonable, but
  the report should not claim XGBoost is the better algorithm.
- The first window (late April to mid May: New Year, Vesak and Adhi Poson) is the hardest: XGBoost is 2.2 portions
  above the floor there, against 0.7 to 1.1 in the other windows. It also has the least training data.

## 5. Feature ablation: do contextual factors help?

XGBoost trained with calendar and history features only, then with holidays, promotions and weather added one group
at a time. The last row is exactly the shipped feature set; its hold-out MAE (9.20) equals the stored model's, which
checks that the experiment and the shipped training agree.

| Feature set | CV MAE (mean +/- std) | windows improved by this step | Hold-out MAE | Hold-out WAPE % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| calendar + history | 10.24 +/- 0.97 | - | 9.96 | 15.7 |
| + holidays (public, Poya, school) | 9.99 +/- 1.13 | 4 of 6 | 9.69 | 15.3 |
| + promotions | **9.36 +/- 0.79** | **6 of 6** | **9.12** | **14.4** |
| + weather (all features, shipped) | 9.45 +/- 0.83 | 3 of 6 | 9.20 | 14.5 |

### Where the errors change (pooled test rows of the six windows, 1,680 item-days)

MAE on the rows where the factor is present, and on all other rows.

| Feature set | holiday days (370) | other days | promotion item-days (46) | other item-days | wet days, actual rain >= 5 mm (670) | dry days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| calendar + history | 11.29 | 9.94 | 32.03 | 9.63 | 10.24 | 10.24 |
| + holidays | 10.74 | 9.77 | 35.19 | 9.28 | 10.24 | 9.82 |
| + promotions | 10.19 | 9.12 | **18.36** | 9.10 | 9.49 | 9.27 |
| + weather | 10.51 | 9.15 | 18.52 | 9.19 | 9.49 | 9.43 |

What this answers (the proposal's question about contextual factors), on synthetic data:

1. **Promotions matter most.** Adding the discount improved every window and almost halved the error on promotion
   item-days (35.2 to 18.4 portions). Without it, a promoted item is badly under-forecast. Promotions are still the
   hardest rows: few examples (46 item-days in six months) and large jumps.
2. **Holidays help, but modestly and not every time** (4 of 6 windows; -0.25 MAE across windows, -0.27 on the
   hold-out). The gain is concentrated on holiday days (11.29 to 10.74). Holiday days remain harder than other days.
3. **Weather adds nothing at forecast time** (3 of 6 windows; +0.09 MAE on average). This is expected rather than
   disappointing: when forecasting, the model only knows the seasonal average for the month, not tomorrow's rain, so
   it cannot tell a wet day from a dry one (wet-day MAE is unchanged at 9.49). Testing the value of weather properly
   needs a real weather forecast as input (postponed: weather API).
4. Feature importance is not a substitute for this test: in the tree, "which menu item" takes 96.7% of the
   importance because items differ ten-fold in volume, and every contextual feature is below 1%, although the ablation
   shows promotions and holidays clearly change the forecasts.

**Not changed:** the shipped model keeps all features, as specified in `ML_PLAN.md`. Dropping weather would lower the
CV MAE slightly (9.36 vs 9.45), but the difference is within the spread between windows, and with a real weather
forecast the feature could become useful. This is a recommendation for future work, not a result to act on.

## 6. Limitations to state in the report

1. Synthetic data: accuracy, and the size of each contextual effect, are not evidence about real restaurants; real
   data can replace the synthetic history through the same CSV import, and the same `evaluate` command then measures it.
2. Sales are not censored by stock-outs in the generator; real sales understate demand when an item sells out.
3. No hyperparameter search; Random Forest and XGBoost use fixed, reasonable settings.
4. No prediction intervals: the safety buffer is a fixed percentage, not derived from forecast uncertainty.
5. Weather is a seasonal average at forecast time; no weather API.
6. The hold-out MAE belongs to the train-only fit; the shipped model was refit on all history (8 more weeks).
7. School-vacation dates are approximate (section 7).

## 7. Holiday calendar

`data/holidays_lk.csv` is built by `scripts/make_holidays.py`. On 4 Oct 2026 the Poya days and movable public
holidays were checked against the government calendars (Government Printing Department desk calendars and gazettes,
Central Bank of Sri Lanka bank-holiday lists, and the published 2025 and 2026 calendars). The earlier version
computed Poya days from the astronomical full moon: 19 of them were one day late (the Poya Committee often declares
the day before the full moon in Colombo time), and five holidays were missing (Milad-un-Nabi 2024, the day after Vesak
in 2025 and 2026, Adhi Poson 2026 and the State Vesak holiday of 31 May 2026); Thai Pongal 2026 was a day early. The
file now lists the declared dates; the synthetic data and the model above were rebuilt with it. School vacations are
still approximate and labelled as such in the file.
